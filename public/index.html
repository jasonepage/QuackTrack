How it works

A team's key is the team. Two taps carry the whole event: one to see the prompt, one to sign the work.

  1. 1. Get a key The organizer hands your team a tagged key from the pool.
  3. 2. Tap to reveal Your prompt is sealed until your key answers. The time is logged.
  5. 3. Tap to sign The key signs the hash of your exact name, repo, commit, and description.
  7. 4. Receipt Anyone with the link sees three checks pass. No login.

Every action is chained

Each log entry carries the hash of the entry before it. Edit one letter anywhere and every later link breaks. The organizer downloads the log and checks it with one command.

Why nobody can cheat

  • The wrong key is refused by the browser

    Each tap allows exactly one credential. Another team's key never reaches the server, and the attempt is logged anyway.

  • A signature locks the exact content

    The signing challenge is built from the hash of the submission plus a fresh nonce. Change a character, or try again later, and it fails.

  • Replays die on arrival

    Every challenge is deleted before it is checked, so a captured request works zero times, even if the signature would have passed.

  • Nothing can be edited quietly

    No edits, no resubmission, no unsigned fallback. A lost key is replaced only by a ceremony that writes OVERRIDE everywhere.

What we store

Your team name, roster handles and roles, your prompt assignment, your submission name, repo, and hash, and log entries.

No prompt content goes in the log. No biometric data is stored. Your key signs on the device, and the private key never leaves it.
